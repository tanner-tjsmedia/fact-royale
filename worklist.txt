  Q: The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST), launched in December 2021, is the most powerful space observatory ever built. What key capability makes it transformative over the Hubble Telescope?
   * JWST observes primarily in infrared light, allowing it to see through dust clouds and observe the universe's earliest galaxies - which are too redshifted for Hubble's optical range to detect
     JWST is the first telescope to directly image black holes, using a gravity lens array that bends light from behind the event horizon
     JWST has 10 times the optical resolution of Hubble, allowing it to distinguish individual stars within galaxies that Hubble could only see as blurry patches
     JWST orbits in the opposite direction from Earth, allowing it to observe objects on the far side of the sun that Hubble could never reach
  E: As the universe expands, light from the most distant (earliest) galaxies is stretched into infrared wavelengths - a phenomenon called cosmological redshift. JWST's infrared instruments let it observe galaxies formed just 300-400 million years after the Big Bang. It also operates at 1.5 million km from Earth (at Lagrange Point 2) - four times the Moon's distance - in permanent shadow of a sunshield the size of a tennis court to stay cold enough for its detectors. Its first images, released in July 2022, revealed galaxy clusters 13 billion light-years away in unprecedented detail.

  Q: In 1998, two independent teams of astronomers studying distant supernovae made a shocking discovery that overturned cosmology. What did they find?
     They discovered that the universe's expansion had been decelerating since the Big Bang, confirming that gravity would eventually cause a 'Big Crunch' - the universe collapsing back on itself
   * They discovered the universe's expansion is accelerating - driven by a mysterious force called 'dark energy' - which implied the universe would expand forever and end in cold darkness
     They discovered that the Milky Way is moving toward the Andromeda galaxy at twice the previously estimated speed, meaning collision would occur in 2 billion years, not 4.5 billion
     They discovered that supernovae in distant galaxies were hotter than predicted, suggesting the universe had been hotter in its early phases than the Big Bang model expected
  E: Saul Perlmutter, Brian Schmidt, and Adam Riess led the two teams using Type Ia supernovae as 'standard candles' to measure cosmic distances. They expected to find the expansion slowing down due to gravity - instead they found it speeding up. Dark energy, the name given to whatever is causing this acceleration, makes up approximately 68% of the universe. Nobody knows what it is. Perlmutter, Schmidt, and Riess shared the 2011 Nobel Prize in Physics for this discovery - one of the most counterintuitive in the history of science.
